Picture a Tuesday night. You are feeding a cat with IBD, and for once the week has gone well: no puddle of sick on the stairs, no mess beside the litter tray. Then you walk into the kitchen and find him with his face in another cat's dish, licking up the last of the ordinary chicken food. You have no idea whether it was one mouthful or ten, or whether it is the first time this week.

In a one-cat home, a special diet is mostly a shopping job. With several cats, it is also a question of access. Every other bowl, dropped piece of kibble and shared treat is a way for the wrong food to get in. This post covers how to set up meals around your vet's plan, and what to track while you wait to see if it is working.

---

## What IBD Means, and Why Diet Comes Up

Cornell's Feline Health Center describes [feline inflammatory bowel disease](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/inflammatory-bowel-disease) as a condition in which a cat's gastrointestinal tract becomes chronically irritated and inflamed. Common signs include vomiting, weight loss, diarrhea, bloody stools, lethargy and decreased appetite. It occurs most often in middle-aged and older cats, though any age can be affected. VCA's page on [IBD in cats](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/inflammatory-bowel-disease-in-cats) calls it a syndrome, despite the name, and says that in most cases an exact cause cannot be identified.

This is not something to diagnose at home. Cornell says a definitive diagnosis requires an intestinal or gastric biopsy. The [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/diseases-of-the-stomach-and-intestines-in-small-animals/chronic-enteropathies-in-small-animals) covers the wider group of long-term gut problems called chronic enteropathies. It says they are diagnosed by excluding other causes, and that telling them apart from intestinal lymphoma in cats is challenging.

Diet is often one part of the plan. Merck reports a marked response to diet change alone in over 50% of patients with chronic enteropathy. That covers a broader group than cats with confirmed IBD, so do not read it as your own cat's odds. Cornell also describes medication used alongside dietary change.

If you are still unsure who is leaving the mess, start with [how to find out which cat is throwing up](/blog/which-cat-is-throwing-up-multi-cat-home).

## What a Diet Trial Asks of a Multi-Cat Home

Follow your vet's choice of food and trial length. Cornell describes hypoallergenic diets with protein or carbohydrate sources the cat has never eaten before. VCA's guide to the [elimination-challenge diet trial](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/implementing-an-elimination-challenge-diet-trial-cat), written about food allergy, describes novel protein diets and hydrolyzed diets, whose proteins are broken into pieces too small for the immune system to recognize. It also asks for a complete list of foods your cat has eaten before, so start writing that down.

### How long it takes

These pages do not give one number, and they are not all describing the same thing. Merck says a hypoallergenic diet should be fed exclusively for at least two weeks to judge whether it works, and that most patients respond within two weeks. Cornell says cats may take several weeks or longer to improve. VCA's food allergy guide says at least eight weeks. Ask which timeline applies to your cat, and put the end date on the calendar.

### "Only this food" really means only

Cornell says that during any food trial, all other food sources must be eliminated, including table scraps, flavored medications and treats. VCA also flags supplements, pet toothpaste and other medications given by mouth, and warns that eating something off the plan can compromise the trial, which may need to start over.

Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own. Ask your vet whether there is a suitable unflavored form or another way to give it. If other cats take medicine too, see [how to medicate a cat without the wrong cat getting the dose](/blog/medicating-cat-multi-cat-home).

Care still matters after the trial. Merck says relapses of chronic enteropathy are most often set off by dietary indiscretion, meaning eating something outside the plan.

## Find Every Leak Before Day One

Think of the trial as carrying water in a bucket: a few small holes are enough to lose it. Walk through your home and look for:

- **Other cats' bowls**, including breakfast leftovers
- **Dry food** that sits out all day
- **Treats** from family members or visitors
- **Counters, plates and the sink**
- **The bin and stored food**, especially torn or open bags
- **Dog bowls**, if you have a dog
- **Other people's food**, if your cat goes outdoors

Then tell everyone in the house. A fridge note helps: the cat's name, the one food allowed, and "ask before feeding." Hand out the other cats' [treats](/blog/how-to-give-treats-to-multiple-cats) behind a closed door.

## How to Feed the IBD Cat Separately

PetMD's guide to [feeding cats separately](https://www.petmd.com/cat/nutrition/feeding-multiple-cats) says cats should ideally be fed separately in a multi-cat household, with stations as far apart as is practical. International Cat Care's [feeding guide](https://icatcare.org/articles/feeding-your-cat-or-kitten) also advises separate areas, away from litter trays, entry points and busy spots.

Here are three options. You can combine the first two.

### 1. Closed-door, timed meals

Feed the IBD cat in one room and the others elsewhere. When the meal window ends, pick up every bowl before you open the door. It needs no new equipment, but someone has to be home for each meal, and grazers need another plan. No spare room? See [whether you really need a separate room](/blog/separate-room-feed-cats-differently).

International Cat Care suggests dividing a cat's daily ration into at least five portions across 24 hours. That is hard to supervise by hand. Ask your vet how many meals suit your cat, then build a schedule you can keep. If you serve wet food, check [how long it can sit out](/blog/how-long-can-wet-cat-food-sit-out-multi-cat-home).

### 2. Selective feeders

PetMD describes microchip or RFID-enabled automatic feeders that open and provide food only when a particular cat approaches.

Think about **which direction you need protection in.** A selective device on the IBD cat's bowl is meant to keep others out of his food. It does nothing to keep him out of theirs. To close that gap, every other bowl he can reach also needs to be restricted to its own cat, or picked up after meals.

### 3. One food for everyone

Putting the whole household on one food sounds like the easy fix. Do not assume a diet chosen for one cat suits the others, especially a kitten or a cat with its own health condition. Ask your vet about every cat first. If you do change the others, International Cat Care advises doing it slowly to avoid digestive upsets. Our guide to [switching food with multiple cats](/blog/switch-cat-food-multiple-cats) covers the practical side.

## If Your IBD Cat Refuses the New Food

Before you start, ask your vet how quickly to move to the new food and what to do if your cat will not eat it.

Do not try to wait him out. Cornell's [feeding advice](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feeding-your-cat) says a cat that refuses to eat can develop serious medical problems. One is a liver disease called [hepatic lipidosis](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/hepatic-lipidosis). Cornell says its onset is almost always accompanied or preceded by loss of appetite, and that it is likely to be fatal if not treated promptly. If your cat is turning down food, call your vet that day.

On hydrolyzed diets, VCA's allergy trial guide notes that cats who do poorly on one often respond better to a different one. That is a reason to talk to your vet about alternatives rather than mixing in the old food.

## Keep a Log You Will Actually Fill In

A few lines a day will help you and your vet see what happened during the trial:

- How much food you put down and how much was left
- Any vomiting, and whether you saw which cat
- Stool changes, if you can tell whose they are
- Weight, checked as often as your vet suggests
- Any slip, with the food and rough time

Write slips down without guilt. They are part of the picture your vet is judging.

Cornell notes that even with proper management, IBD symptoms may come and go. It also says IBD can often be controlled so that cats are healthy and comfortable, and that keeping it in check requires strict compliance with dietary and medical therapies.

## A Hypothetical Setup for Three Cats

Picture a home where Otis is on a vet-chosen trial food, while Fern and Juno eat their usual diet. Juno likes to graze.

The owner stops leaving kibble out. Otis eats in the bedroom; Fern and Juno eat in the kitchen. Every bowl is picked up before the doors open. Juno gets extra small meals while someone can supervise. The fridge note goes up, with the log beside it.

It is not elegant, but it closes the most obvious gaps.

## When This Won't Work

- **Your cat is very unwell.** Merck's sequence of treatment trials does not apply in the same way to patients who are debilitated, not eating or losing weight severely. A cat like this needs prompt veterinary care, not a new feeding routine.
- **Diet alone is not enough.** Separate feeding cannot replace medication or further tests. If signs continue or return, go back to your vet.
- **Your cat hunts or visits other houses.** An indoor setup cannot control what he eats elsewhere. Ask your vet how to handle this during the trial.
- **The household is not on board.** If someone keeps slipping him treats or leaving bowls out, equipment will not fix it.
- **Your cat is wary of a device.** He may hesitate to eat near a moving lid. Introduce it gradually, ideally before a trial starts, using [these steps for a cat afraid of a feeder lid](/blog/cat-afraid-of-feeder-lid), and check that he is still eating.

## FAQ

### Can my other cats eat my IBD cat's food?

Ask your vet, because the answer depends on the food and on each cat. Stolen mouthfuls can also mean your IBD cat misses part of a meal, and your intake notes will be wrong.

### What if my cat with IBD eats another cat's food once?

It may matter. VCA's diet trial guide warns that eating something off the plan can compromise a trial, which may need to restart. Note what was eaten and when, and tell your vet. Call promptly if your cat seems unwell or stops eating.

### Can a cat with IBD have treats?

Not during a food trial. Cornell lists treats among the other food sources that must be eliminated. After the trial, ask your vet which options fit your cat's plan.
