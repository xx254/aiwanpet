Picture the sofa on a quiet evening. Three cats are sprawled along it: a sleek grey one, a ginger who seems to be turning into a loaf, and an older tabby whose spine you can feel when you stroke her. They all eat from the same kitchen, on the same schedule, and you have a vague feeling that the food is not landing evenly. But "vague feeling" is not something you can take to a vet, and it is not something you can plan meals around.

A body condition check is the fix. It takes about a minute per cat, needs no equipment, and gives each cat its own number. In a multi-cat home, that number matters more than usual, because the cats share a food supply but don't share a waistline.

This guide shows how to check each cat's body condition at home, how to read what you find, and how to keep notes that a vet can use.

---

## Why the Scale Alone Isn't Enough

Most owners reach for the bathroom scale first. It helps, but it only tells part of the story. The Association for Pet Obesity Prevention (APOP) says on its [cat body condition page](https://petobesityprevention.org/catbcs) that "weight alone cannot show how much of your cat's weight is fat," and that "the same weight can represent different body conditions depending on frame, body type, age, and muscle mass." It adds that a scale can't show whether a change came from fat, muscle, or both.

That matters in a mixed household. Two cats can weigh the same and look completely different. Weight is useful for tracking change over time, but the hands-on check tells you what the weight is made of.

It is also worth doing because the problem is common and easy to miss. APOP's [2022 survey](https://www.petobesityprevention.org/articlesandnews/new-survey-reveals-alarming-rates-of-pet-obesity-in-the-us) reported that 61% of cats were classified as overweight or having obesity. When that many cats are above ideal, a ginger loaf can start to look normal.

## The Body Condition Score in Plain Terms

Vets score cats on a nine-point scale. APOP says that "for most adult cats, BCS 5/9 is the usual target." The rough guide, from the same page:

- **Score 5 (ideal):** ribs easy to feel beneath a thin fat layer, waist visible behind the ribs, minimal abdominal fat.
- **Score 6 (above ideal):** more fat cover, and the waist or belly tuck is less distinct.
- **Score 7 to 9 (markedly above ideal):** ribs difficult to feel, waist reduced or absent, more fat deposits.
- **Scores below 5:** leaner than ideal, with the lowest scores meaning very thin.

APOP also notes that in some cats, especially older cats, the veterinary team may decide that 6/9 is acceptable. So treat your own score as a starting point for a conversation, not a verdict.

## How to Check a Cat at Home

Do this when the cat is calm, and use the same checks every time.

### 1. Feel the ribs

Use light pressure along both sides of the chest. APOP describes the ideal as ribs that are "easy to feel beneath a thin, even layer of fat, but they do not feel sharp." If you have to press firmly, or can't find the ribs at all, that suggests more fat cover.

A familiar comparison: VCA's [obesity in cats](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/obesity-in-cats) page suggests comparing the feel of the ribs to your own hand, and says this is a good way to measure progress between formal weigh-ins.

### 2. Look down from above

Stand over the cat and look along the back. APOP says to "look for a visible inward curve behind the ribs." A broad, rounded outline can indicate increased fat cover.

### 3. Look from the side

From the side, you want to see a slight abdominal tuck. One caution from APOP: don't judge belly shape alone, because "a primordial pouch can be normal." Many cats have a loose flap of skin under the belly that swings when they walk. That is not the same as fat over the ribs, which is why the rib check comes first.

### 4. Write it down

A phone note is enough. For each cat: the date, a score out of nine, the weight, and one line on what you felt. Do it once a month, on the same day, and the pattern builds itself.

## Making It Work When the Cats Won't Cooperate

Not every cat wants to be examined. A few things help.

- **Do one cat at a time.** A cat who watches another being handled tends to hover or bolt.
- **Pick a quiet moment.** After a nap is better than before breakfast, when the hungry cat is a moving target.
- **Keep it short.** If a cat tenses, stop and try again tomorrow.
- **Reward with something small.** VCA says treats should be no more than 10% of a cat's daily calorie intake, so keep the reward tiny.
- **Ask your vet to demonstrate.** At the next visit, ask them to show you what a 5 feels like on your cat. Your hands will learn faster than any chart.

## What to Do With the Results

### One cat is above ideal

Don't just serve a smaller scoop of the same food. VCA says "it is not appropriate to simply reduce the volume of your cat's current food," and recommends a calorie-restricted therapeutic diet with set portions and meal frequency. That plan should come from your vet. Our guide on [helping an overweight cat lose weight when you have multiple cats](/blog/overweight-cat-diet-multiple-cats) covers the household side, and [cat portion control in multi-cat households](/blog/cat-portion-control-multiple-cats) compares the ways of keeping portions accurate.

Weight loss needs supervision. VCA warns that obese cats who stop eating are at significant risk of hepatic lipidosis, which it describes as a potentially life-threatening liver condition. If a cat on a plan stops eating, call the vet promptly rather than waiting to see what happens.

VCA also says monthly weigh-ins are important for successful weight loss. That lines up neatly with a monthly body condition check.

### One cat is below ideal

A leaner score deserves a vet visit, especially if it is new. If the cat is thin because she is losing out at mealtimes, the answer is protected meals rather than more food in the common bowl. See [feeding an underweight cat when another cat is on a diet](/blog/feeding-underweight-cat-multi-cat-home) for how to set that up.

### Everyone is the same score

That is a good sign, but it doesn't mean everything is fine. Keep checking. A household that matches today can drift apart over a few months.

## Where the Food Is Going

If the scores are uneven, the next question is why. Common reasons in shared homes:

- **One cat eats faster and finishes first.** Then she moves on to the next bowl.
- **A cat who is on a diet is eating a thinner cat's food.** Our post on [why your cat is stealing the other cat's food](/blog/cat-stealing-other-cats-food) explains how that starts.
- **A cat who always seems hungry.** See [why is my cat always hungry](/blog/why-is-my-cat-always-hungry) for how to tell appetite from a medical problem.

Think of it as a shared pantry. If everyone can reach everything, the quickest cats take the most, and the numbers on the scale tell you the result rather than the cause.

### Ways to keep each cat to her own portion

- **Fed in separate rooms with doors shut.** Free and reliable, but you need to be home and supervise every meal.
- **Timed meals with bowls picked up afterwards.** Simple, but it won't stop a fast cat from racing across the room.
- **Elevated or separate feeding spots.** Works if the cats' abilities differ, such as a high shelf for the one who jumps.
- **A selective feeder that opens only for the right cat.** This removes the supervision, but it has limits, covered below.

---

## When This Won't Work

A hands-on score is a rough tool. Thick coats can make ribs harder to read, and some cats simply won't tolerate being handled. Your first scores may also be off by a point either way, and that's fine: the trend over several months matters more than any single number. If a score changes quickly with no change in meals, that is a medical question, not a feeding one, and no feeding set-up will fix an underlying illness. A selective feeder also only controls access to food. It doesn't choose the portion, so you still need the vet's numbers.

## FAQ

### How do I know if my cat is overweight in a multi-cat home?

Check each cat by hand. Use light pressure along the sides of the chest: ribs that need firm pressure, or can't be felt, suggest more fat cover. From above, look for a curve inward behind the ribs, and from the side, look for a slight tuck in the belly. Compare each cat with her own previous notes rather than with the others.

### What is a healthy body condition score for a cat?

According to APOP, 5 out of 9 is the usual target for most adult cats. Your vet may decide a 6 is acceptable in some cats, especially older ones.

### Can I tell if my cat is overweight just by weighing her?

Not reliably. APOP says weight alone can't show how much of a cat's weight is fat, because frame, body type, age and muscle mass all matter. Use the scale to track change, and the hands-on check to judge condition.

### How often should I check my cats?

Monthly works well. VCA notes that monthly weigh-ins are important for successful weight loss, and a quick hands-on check on the same day keeps your notes in one place.

### Can I put an overweight cat on a diet without a vet?

It's best not to. VCA advises against simply cutting the volume of the current food, and warns about the liver risk if an obese cat stops eating. Ask your vet to set the plan and the target.
