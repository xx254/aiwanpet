It's 5:40 a.m. and someone is standing on your chest. Not the whole household, just one cat: the grey one, who ate a full breakfast yesterday, a full dinner last night, and still spent the evening following you to the fridge and shouting at the cupboard door. The other two are asleep at the foot of the bed. Their bowls were empty this morning as well. You honestly can't say who emptied them.

If you're asking "why is my cat always hungry," the answer could be a medical problem, a habit, or the plain fact that this cat isn't getting the food you think it's getting. In a single-cat home you can usually tell which. With several cats, the signals blur. One cat's constant begging might be an overactive thyroid. It might be a cat whose dinner is being eaten by a housemate. It might be a cat who has learned that [yelling at 5:40 a.m.](/blog/cat-wakes-me-up-for-food) works.

This guide covers the common causes, how to tell real hunger from learned begging, and how to find out what each cat is actually eating before you change anything.

---

## Is It Real Hunger or Learned Begging?

Start with this question, because the two need very different responses.

Wild cats spend a lot of time finding food. The AAFP and ISFM consensus statement on [feline feeding programs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11343346) notes that "cats can naturally spend half of every 24 h looking for and obtaining food," and that cats "naturally prefer to eat small, frequent meals alone." An indoor cat fed two big bowls a day has a lot of spare time and energy. Some of it goes into asking you for food.

The same statement says that splitting the daily allowance into "multiple small meals" fed through the day "diminishes begging for food, feline frustration and inter-cat conflict." Put another way, some begging comes from how and when the food arrives, not from how much of it there is.

Learned begging tends to look like this:

- It happens at predictable times: when you wake up, when you walk into the kitchen, when you open any cupboard.
- It stops once you give attention, play, or a treat, even a tiny one.
- The cat's weight is stable or creeping up.
- Nothing else about the cat has changed.

Real hunger, the kind worth a vet visit, tends to look different:

- It is new, or noticeably worse than it was a few months ago.
- The cat eats more but holds the same weight or loses it.
- It comes with other changes: drinking more, peeing more, vomiting, a scruffy coat, restlessness.

The second list matters most. **A cat who eats more and gets thinner needs to see a vet.** That pattern is the classic sign of several common diseases.

## Medical Reasons a Cat Is Always Hungry

The UK veterinary charity PDSA lists the main [medical causes of a cat always being hungry](https://www.pdsa.org.uk/pet-help-and-advice/pet-health-hub/symptoms/why-is-my-cat-always-hungry): hyperthyroidism, diabetes, parasites such as worms, certain medications "such as steroids and anti-seizure medications," pregnancy and lactation, malabsorption, and rarer hormonal conditions such as acromegaly and Cushing's disease. Their advice is direct: "Contact your vet if your cat has been hungrier than usual or is looking for food all the time."

The two you are most likely to meet in a multi-cat home with older cats are below.

### Hyperthyroidism

Cornell's Feline Health Center describes [hyperthyroidism](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/hyperthyroidism-cats) as "a common disease in cats" that "mostly afflicts cats middle-aged and older." The center says: "The most common clinical signs of hyperthyroidism are weight loss, increased appetite, and increased thirst and urination." Vomiting, diarrhoea, hyperactivity and an unkempt or greasy coat can also appear.

The hyperactivity is worth noticing. A hyperthyroid cat often doesn't look ill. It looks busy, vocal and keen on food, which is easy to mistake for a cheerful older cat with a big appetite. If that's your situation, our guide to [managing a hyperthyroid cat alongside other cats](/blog/hyperthyroid-cat-multiple-cats) covers the feeding side once you have a diagnosis.

### Diabetes

Cornell's page on [feline diabetes](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feline-diabetes) explains that when cells can't use glucose, the body breaks down fat and protein instead, and "this breakdown results in weight loss, despite an increased appetite." It also notes that "excessive thirst and urination can also signal diabetes in a cat." In a house with shared litter boxes, the clue is often heavier, wetter clumps that you can't pin to one cat. For feeding once a cat is diagnosed, see [Diabetic Cat Diet in a Multi-Cat Household](/blog/diabetic-cat-diet-multiple-cats).

### Worms, malabsorption and medication

Worms and malabsorption both mean food goes in but less gets used, so the cat stays hungry. Steroids are a common cause of a sudden big appetite. If your cat started prednisolone recently and is now raiding the counters, mention it to your vet. It may be an expected side effect, but they'll want to know.

## Everyday Reasons That Aren't Disease

PDSA also lists non-medical causes: "boredom or stress for example the introduction of a new pet," a change to a food that "doesn't provide a balanced diet," more exercise, such as an indoor cat starting to go outdoors, and a colder environment, "for example in Winter." Some of these are temporary. A cat that has started going out, or is getting through a cold month, may simply need a bit more food. Ask your vet before you increase portions.

Two more reasons come up a lot in multi-cat homes.

### The treats add up and nobody keeps count

Cornell advises that treats shouldn't exceed "10 to 15 percent of a cat's daily caloric intake." Now picture a three-person household where everyone gives the loud cat "just one" treat to make it stop. The begging has worked three times before lunch, so the cat does it again.

### The cat is overweight, and you can't see it

The Association for Pet Obesity Prevention's 2022 survey found that ["61% of cats were classified as overweight or having obesity"](https://www.petobesityprevention.org/2022) by veterinary professionals. The same survey found that many owners of overweight pets rated them as normal or ideal. A cat who always seems hungry and also has a soft belly and no visible waist may not need more food. It may need a plan. Our guide to [helping an overweight cat lose weight with multiple cats](/blog/overweight-cat-diet-multiple-cats) walks through that.

## The Multi-Cat Reason: It May Not Be Eating Its Own Food

This is the cause that's easy to miss, and it works in both directions.

**The hungry cat is losing its food.** A quiet or older cat steps up to its bowl, a pushier cat arrives, and the quiet one backs off. The AAFP statement notes that "competition for food can cause conflict and pre-feeding aggression," and that cats "may not show overt signs of tension or stress, which therefore often go unrecognized by owners." Cornell lists "the presence of other animals" among the things that can stop a cat from eating. So the cat crying at you at 5:40 a.m. may really be hungry, because last night's dinner went to someone else.

**The hungry cat is eating everyone's food, and still asks for more.** Some cats finish their own bowl in a minute and then clear the others. Those cats get used to large meals, and the one bowl you put down for them no longer seems like enough.

Think of a shared office fridge. Everyone labelled their lunch, the fridge is empty by two o'clock, and nobody admits to anything. You can't fix the problem until you know who is eating what. Our posts on [why a cat steals the other cat's food](/blog/cat-stealing-other-cats-food) and the [signs a cat is eating another cat's food](/blog/signs-cat-eating-another-cats-food) go into this in more detail.

## How to Work Out Which One It Is

Take a week. Treat it as gathering information, not as a fix.

### 1. Weigh every cat

Use a baby scale, or weigh yourself holding the cat and subtract your own weight. Write it down, then weigh again in two weeks. A cat who is always hungry and losing weight needs a vet appointment, whatever else is happening. Bring the numbers with you.

### 2. Stop free feeding

You can't see who eats what from a bowl that's out all day. Move to set meals at set times. If that's a big change for your household, [Best Feeding Schedule for Multiple Cats](/blog/best-feeding-schedule-multiple-cats) has sample schedules. Following the AAFP guidance, three or four smaller meals tend to cause less begging than two large ones.

### 3. Feed each cat in its own spot and watch

Put bowls far enough apart that no cat can watch another eat, or in separate rooms. Stay for the meal. Note who finishes, who walks away, and who goes looking for a second bowl. Pick bowls up when each cat is done.

### 4. Put all treats in one place, with one person in charge

Measure the daily treats in the morning into a small jar. When the jar is empty, there are no more treats until tomorrow. This also shows you how much begging was being rewarded.

### 5. Keep a simple log

Each cat, each meal: ate all, ate some, didn't eat. Add water and litter notes if you can. After a week, the pattern is usually clear. Either the hungry cat eats its full portion and still begs, which points to habit or a medical cause, or it isn't getting its full portion, which points to competition.

### 6. Add something to do between meals

Puzzle feeders and food hidden around the house give a cat something to do with the hours it would otherwise spend hunting. The AAFP statement notes these increase activity and provide "mental and physical stimulation." Use part of the cat's daily allowance for this, not extra food. If the hungry cat also eats too fast, [How to Stop a Cat From Eating Too Fast](/blog/stop-cat-eating-too-fast) has more options.

## When This Won't Work

A week of logs won't diagnose anything. If a cat is eating more and losing weight, or drinking and peeing more, skip the experiment and book the vet. Blood tests answer that question faster than any feeding plan. Some households also can't feed at separate times or supervise meals. Shift workers, homes where several people feed the cats, and cats that are already fighting at the bowl are all hard cases. Changing the schedule won't calm a home where mealtimes are already tense. And some cats really are just loud about food. If the vet has ruled out medical causes and the weight is stable, the begging may never go away completely. You can only stop rewarding it.

## FAQ

### Why is my cat always hungry but losing weight?

Eating more while losing weight is a common sign of hyperthyroidism and diabetes, according to Cornell's Feline Health Center. Worms and malabsorption can do the same. See your vet; it usually needs a physical exam and blood tests.

### Is my cat actually hungry or just begging?

If the begging happens at predictable times, stops when the cat gets attention, and the cat's weight is steady or rising, it's probably learned. If it's new, getting worse, or comes with weight loss or extra thirst, treat it as a possible medical problem.

### Should I just feed my always-hungry cat more?

Not until you know why it's hungry. If an overweight cat is begging, more food makes the weight worse. If the cat's food is being stolen, more food in the same bowl will be stolen too. Weigh the cat, watch who eats what, and ask your vet before changing portions.

### Can a cat be hungry because the other cats eat its food?

Yes. In multi-cat homes a quieter cat may back away from its bowl when a bolder cat arrives, often with no visible fight. The only way to know is to feed cats separately and watch each one eat.

### How many times a day should I feed a cat that's always hungry?

The AAFP recommends splitting the daily food allowance into multiple small meals spread through the day, which it says reduces begging. Keep the total amount the same and serve it in more meals.
