You put three bowls down at seven. By ten past, all three are licked clean, and everything looks normal. Then you pick up the tabby to move her off the laptop and she feels lighter than you remember. Her hip bones are easier to find. She was at the bowls this morning, wasn't she? You saw three cats in the kitchen. You didn't see who ate what.

That's the hard part when your cat isn't eating and there are other cats in the house. A cat who lives alone and stops eating leaves a full bowl behind. In a multi-cat home, the bowl gets emptied anyway. Someone else finishes it, and the cat who skipped the meal can go days without anyone noticing.

This guide covers why a cat not eating is more urgent than it looks, how to find out which cat is skipping meals, the common reasons it happens in a shared home, and what to do once you know.

---

## Why a Cat Not Eating Can't Wait

For us a skipped meal is a small thing. For cats it is more serious, because of a liver condition called hepatic lipidosis, or fatty liver disease.

Cornell's Feline Health Center describes [hepatic lipidosis](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/hepatic-lipidosis) as a "frequently diagnosed" condition that "can severely impair the health and proper functioning of a cat's liver and, if not promptly reversed, can be fatal." Cornell notes the incidence "seems much higher in obese cats."

VCA's page on [fatty liver syndrome in cats](https://vcahospitals.com/all-caring/know-your-pet/liver-disease-fatty-liver-syndrome-in-cats) says cats with the condition have usually gone without food for "three to four consecutive days," and that the risk is greater "if the cat was overweight or obese before the anorexia began." It also says that "in greater than 90% of cats, an underlying disease has caused the inappetence." So the not-eating is usually a symptom of something else, and the liver problem comes on top of it.

The University of Illinois vet school makes the practical point in its column on [hepatic lipidosis when cats don't eat](https://vetmed.illinois.edu/pet-health-columns/hepatic-lipidosis-when-cats-dont-eat/): "not eating for multiple days" is the sign to watch for.

Now picture a multi-cat home. A few days in a row is exactly how long a quiet cat can go unnoticed when the bowls keep coming back empty.

## Common Reasons a Cat Stops Eating

The UK veterinary charity PDSA lists the usual causes on its page [my cat isn't eating](https://www.pdsa.org.uk/pet-help-and-advice/pet-health-hub/symptoms/my-cats-not-eating). They fall into two groups.

### Medical causes

- **Teeth and gums.** PDSA notes that "tooth and gum disease is painful and can put cats off eating." A cat with a sore mouth may walk up to the bowl, sniff, and walk off.
- **Kidney disease**, which PDSA says "often causes vomiting, increased thirst and a reduced appetite."
- **Pain and fever**, including pain you can't see.
- **Other illness**: PDSA names heart disease, pancreatitis, liver disease and cancer.

PDSA's advice is short: "Contact your vet for an appointment if your cat stops eating."

### Stress and change

PDSA also lists stress, with examples such as a change in routine, moving home, or fighting with another cat. It warns that "changing your cat's food suddenly or moving their bowl from its usual place may stop them eating."

That last point catches a lot of multi-cat households. You moved the bowls to stop one cat stealing, or switched everyone to a new food, and the cat who was already a bit nervous quietly stopped eating. If you've recently changed diets, our guide on [how to switch cat food when you have multiple cats](/blog/switch-cat-food-multiple-cats) covers doing it gradually.

## The Multi-Cat Reason: Competition at the Bowl

One cause mostly happens in shared homes, and it's easy to miss because it rarely looks like a fight.

The AAFP and ISFM consensus statement on [feline feeding programs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11343346) says cats "prefer to eat often and alone," and that "competition for food can cause conflict and pre-feeding aggression." It adds that "cats may not show overt signs of tension or stress."

In practice, a confident cat finishes its own bowl, then walks over and stands near the quiet cat's bowl. Nobody hisses. The quiet cat steps back and leaves. A minute later both bowls are empty, and from the doorway dinner looks like it went well.

It's like a school lunch table where one kid always "helps" finish everyone's tray. The teacher sees empty trays and assumes everyone ate. You only find out by sitting at the table.

## How to Find Out Which Cat Isn't Eating

You don't need special equipment. You need a few days and a notebook.

### 1. Weigh everyone today

Use a baby scale, or weigh yourself holding each cat and subtract your own weight. Write the numbers down with the date. Your vet will want them.

### 2. Stop free feeding, at least for now

A bowl that sits out all day hides everything. Switch to set meals at set times and pick the bowls up afterwards. For a schedule to start from, see [Best Feeding Schedule for Multiple Cats](/blog/best-feeding-schedule-multiple-cats).

### 3. Separate the cats at mealtimes

Feed each cat in its own spot, out of sight of the others: different rooms, or behind a closed door. The AAFP statement advises that cats "should be fed in locations where they feel safe" and that feeding stations "should not be close to litter boxes."

### 4. Measure what goes in and what's left

Weigh or measure each portion. After the meal, check each bowl before anyone else can reach it. With wet food, weigh the bowl before and after on a kitchen scale.

### 5. Watch how each cat eats, not just whether

A cat who approaches eagerly and then backs away may have a sore mouth. A cat who eats happily alone but not with others nearby is telling you about competition. A cat who won't approach the bowl at all, even alone, needs a vet visit soon.

### 6. Compare after a few days

Usually a pattern appears. Either the quiet cat eats normally when it's alone, which points to competition, or it doesn't eat well even alone, which points to a medical cause. In the second case, stop experimenting and call your vet.

## When to Call the Vet Straight Away

Don't wait for a pattern if you see any of these:

- A cat that has eaten little or nothing for a day or more. You may not know when the skipping actually started, so you may already be further into VCA's three-to-four-day window than you think.
- A cat that was overweight before it stopped eating. VCA and Cornell both flag these cats as higher risk.
- Yellowing of the gums, ears, or the whites of the eyes. VCA notes that affected cats usually become "icteric or jaundiced."
- Hiding, a hunched posture, vomiting, or drinking much more than usual.

Bring your weight log and meal notes. "She's lost a bit of weight" is hard for a vet to work with. "She was 4.6 kg on the 3rd and 4.3 kg today, and walks away from wet food after two bites" is far more useful.

## Once You Know: Making Sure the Right Cat Eats

If the cause is medical, your vet leads on treatment, and you may need to feed a special diet or track exact amounts. That's very hard when the other cats can reach the bowl. Our guide to [prescription cat food in a multi-cat home](/blog/prescription-cat-food-multiple-cats) covers the practical side.

If the cause is competition, give the quiet cat a meal nobody else can get to. Your options:

- **Separate rooms with closed doors** at every meal. Free and effective, but someone has to be home to manage each meal.
- **A high feeding spot** only the quiet cat uses. This only works if the others can't or won't jump up there.
- **A selective feeder** that opens only for the cat it's set up for. The AAFP statement mentions feeders that "utilize individual microchips" as one option. Our explainer on [whether microchip feeders actually work](/blog/do-microchip-cat-feeders-work) covers the trade-offs.

Whichever you choose, keep weighing for a few weeks to confirm the quiet cat is actually eating.

---

## When This Won't Work

None of this replaces a vet. Separating cats at mealtimes tells you whether competition is part of the problem. It can't tell you about kidney values, a bad tooth, or anything else going on inside the cat. Some cats also eat less when their routine changes, so a new setup can look worse for a day or two. That is not a reason to keep going if the cat really isn't eating. And a private feeding spot won't help a cat whose appetite is gone because it feels unwell. If your cat won't eat even when it's alone and safe, assume the cause is medical.

## FAQ

### How long can a cat go without eating?

Not long. VCA notes that cats with fatty liver disease have usually gone without food for three to four consecutive days, and overweight cats are at greater risk. PDSA advises contacting your vet if your cat stops eating.

### How do I know which cat isn't eating when all the bowls are empty?

Feed each cat separately, out of sight of the others, at set times. Measure the food going in and check what's left before another cat can reach it. Weigh each cat now and again a week later.

### Can another cat stop my cat from eating without fighting?

Yes. The AAFP and ISFM feeding consensus notes that cats often don't show obvious signs of tension. A quieter cat may simply step away when a more confident cat comes over.

### My cat eats when it's alone but not with the other cats. Is that normal?

It's common. Cats prefer to eat alone, according to the AAFP. If your cat eats well in a separate room and holds its weight, a private feeding spot for every meal may be all it needs.
