You open the fridge to get the milk and suddenly there are three cats in the kitchen. One is shouting. One is sitting on your foot. The third has climbed onto the counter and is watching the kettle as if it might be hiding a pouch of tuna. You have fed them all within the last hour. You have not even touched their food. You just walked into the room.

If your cats beg for food at every opportunity, it is exhausting, and with several cats it is worse, because begging spreads. One cat learns that shouting works, the others watch, and before long the whole house is doing it. This post is about why cats beg, why a multi-cat home makes it harder to stop, and a routine that tends to calm things down without leaving anyone hungry.

## Why Cats Beg for Food

### Begging often gets results

The plainest explanation is that it has been rewarded. At some point a cat cried, you gave a few biscuits to get some peace, and the cat learned that crying produces food. It does not matter that you did it once, on a bad morning. From the cat's side, the lesson looks clear.

In a multi-cat home that lesson is shared. The cats see each other getting results, and a cat that never used to beg may start, because the others seem to be doing well out of it.

### The routine is unpredictable

If meals come at roughly the same times and in the same place, a cat has less reason to keep checking whether food is on its way. If meals shift with your mood or your work calendar, every footstep in the kitchen becomes a possible meal. [Best feeding schedule for multiple cats](/blog/best-feeding-schedule-multiple-cats) covers how to set a rhythm that suits a busy household.

### The meals are too few and too easy

International Cat Care's guidance on [feeding your cat or kitten](https://icatcare.org/articles/feeding-your-cat-or-kitten) says a cat's daily ration should be divided into at least five portions across 24 hours, using timed and puzzle feeders where possible. It also says that when cats eat from the same bowl in the same place every day, "this lack of mental stimulation can lead to boredom, anxiety, frustration and stress," especially in indoor cats. A cat who gets two bowls a day and has nothing else to do may put real energy into getting you to produce more.

That does not mean every begging cat is bored. It does mean a long gap between two big meals is a plausible driver, and one you can change.

### Sometimes it is not behaviour at all

A cat that suddenly begs much more than usual, or that eats well and still loses weight, may have a medical problem. Hunger that does not match the amount eaten is worth a vet visit before you try to train it away. [Why is my cat always hungry](/blog/why-is-my-cat-always-hungry) goes through what to look for and what to tell the vet.

## Why a Multi-Cat Home Makes Begging Harder to Fix

### You cannot tell who is actually underfed

With one cat, you can check the bowl and the waistline. With three, a loud cat may simply be loud. A quiet cat who ate half her dinner before being chased off may be the genuinely hungry one. If you are not sure which is which, [how to check each cat's body condition](/blog/cat-body-condition-score-multi-cat-home) gives you a hands-on check that does not depend on volume.

### One cat's begging triggers the others

When you finally cave and give one cat a treat, the other two arrive at a run. Now you are handing out three treats, the portion plan is out the window, and everybody has seen that the noise worked. Extras add up: the Association for Pet Obesity Prevention's 2022 survey found that [61% of cats were classified as overweight or having obesity](https://www.petobesityprevention.org/articlesandnews/new-survey-reveals-alarming-rates-of-pet-obesity-in-the-us).

### Mealtimes themselves can be tense

International Cat Care notes that cats generally do not like to share food bowls or be seen by other cats when eating, and suggests feeding them in separate areas. A cat who has been pushed off the bowl, or who has learned to gulp before another cat arrives, may beg afterwards because she did not really finish. [My cat gets chased away from the food bowl](/blog/cat-chased-away-from-food-bowl-multi-cat-home) covers that situation.

## A Calm Routine That Reduces Begging

Nothing here is instant. Expect a few weeks of consistency, and expect it to feel worse before it feels better, because a cat who has been rewarded for shouting may well try shouting harder first.

### Step 1: Rule out the medical and the portion causes

Before you change anything, check that the cat is not losing weight, drinking far more than usual, vomiting or acting differently. If so, call your vet. Then check that each cat is getting an appropriate portion. [How much should I feed my cat](/blog/how-much-to-feed-each-cat-multi-cat-home) helps you work out a starting point to confirm with your vet.

### Step 2: Fix the meal pattern first

Put meals on a predictable schedule, with the same times and places each day. If your cats are on two large meals, ask your vet whether splitting the same daily amount into more, smaller meals suits them, in line with the International Cat Care advice above. The total stays the same. Only the spacing changes.

### Step 3: Make some of the food take effort

A puzzle feeder or a scatter of kibble gives the cat something to do other than follow you. International Cat Care advises introducing puzzle feeders gradually, starting easy and keeping part of the meal in a normal bowl at first. In a multi-cat home they have their own quirks, which [puzzle feeders for multiple cats](/blog/puzzle-feeders-multiple-cats) covers.

### Step 4: Stop paying for the shouting

This is the hard part. Decide, as a household, that begging does not produce food. That means no tidbit from the counter, no "just this once" at 6 a.m., and no giving in when the cat is at its loudest. Everyone in the home has to agree, because one soft touch undoes the rest.

Some ways to make it bearable:

- Keep regular meal times, so that waiting has a clear end.
- Try not to scold. Shouting or shooing is still attention.
- Notice the quiet moment. When a cat is calm, that is when she gets a stroke, play or her planned meal.
- Use a wand toy for a few minutes before a meal to give the energy somewhere to go.

If the kitchen is the trigger, feed the cats somewhere else and, if you can, shut the kitchen door while you cook.

### Step 5: Count the treats

Treats are the usual leak in the plan. [How to give treats to multiple cats](/blog/how-to-give-treats-to-multiple-cats) covers how to hand them out without fights, and [cat portion control in multi-cat households](/blog/cat-portion-control-multiple-cats) compares ways to keep each cat's total where the vet wants it.

## A Hypothetical Evening

Picture a three-cat flat. Mochi is a confident young cat who yowls the moment anyone enters the kitchen. Juniper is a quiet senior who eats slowly. Biscuit copies whatever Mochi does. The owner has been giving in with a few biscuits "to keep the peace," so all three now appear whenever the cupboard door opens.

She sets three meals at fixed times, moves part of the evening kibble into a puzzle feeder, and starts a household rule that nothing is handed out in the kitchen. Mochi's yowling gets louder for the first week. By the third, Biscuit has mostly stopped, and Juniper is eating at her own pace in the bedroom. It is invented, but it is the shape of how this tends to go: noisier first, then quieter.

## When This Won't Work

- **A medical cause will not be fixed by routine.** If a cat is always hungry but losing weight, or her appetite changed suddenly, see your vet first.
- **It needs every person in the house.** If one family member keeps feeding on demand, the cats learn that shouting still works with that person.
- **It does not solve who eats what.** A better routine will not stop a food-motivated cat from raiding another cat's bowl, particularly if one cat is on a prescription diet. See [how to feed multiple cats separately](/blog/how-to-feed-multiple-cats-separately).
- **Some cats are simply chatty.** Not every cry is a demand for food.
- **The first week can be loud.** Giving in halfway teaches the cat to try harder next time.

## FAQ

### Why does my cat beg for food right after eating?

There are several possible reasons: the meal was small or eaten too fast, another cat interrupted her, begging has been rewarded before, or there is a medical cause. If it is new or the cat is losing weight, check with your vet. If mealtimes are tense, separate feeding spots may help.

### Should I ignore a cat that begs for food?

Not giving food in response to begging, while keeping to a predictable meal schedule, is the usual starting point. Do not ignore a cat who seems unwell, and do not skip planned meals.

### How many times a day should I feed my cats?

International Cat Care's guidance is to divide the daily ration into at least five portions across 24 hours, using timed and puzzle feeders where possible. Check with your vet what that means for each cat's total, particularly for cats with health conditions.

### Is begging a sign my cat is overweight?

Not necessarily, but extra treats and snacks can contribute to weight gain. It is worth checking each cat's body condition rather than guessing.

### Can I stop only one cat from begging?

You can try, but in a multi-cat home the others will notice what works. It is easier to apply the same rules to everyone and let each cat have her own portion at her own spot.
