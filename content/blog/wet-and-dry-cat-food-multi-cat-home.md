Saturday morning, the pouch rips open and three cats appear from three different rooms. The old tabby gets a saucer of wet food by the radiator. The two young cats have a bowl of kibble each, which they inhale in about a minute, and then both of them walk over to the saucer. The tabby leans away from his food and lets them. By the time you have finished your coffee, the saucer is clean and you honestly cannot say who ate it.

If you feed wet food to one cat and dry food to the others, or you simply want to give everyone a mix, this scene is probably familiar. This post covers what wet and dry food actually differ on, whether you can feed both in one home, and how to stop the "wrong" cat eating the "wrong" bowl.

## Wet vs Dry Cat Food: What Actually Differs

The big difference is water. Cornell's Feline Health Center says [dry food contains between six and 10 percent water, while canned cat food has a moisture content of at least 75 percent](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feeding-your-cat). Its hydration page adds that [cats that eat wet food, which can contain up to 80% water, may drink less, and cats that eat dry food usually take more of their daily water in by drinking](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/hydration).

VCA lists a few other trade-offs in its guide to [dry, canned and semi-moist foods](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/dry-canned-or-semi-moist-food-choices-for-cats):

- Canned food has a very high water content, which can help increase a cat's water intake, but it is more expensive per calorie than kibble.
- Some kibble diets carry oral health claims, whereas no canned food options carry dental health benefits.
- Dry food suits cats that prefer to graze through the day.

Neither is automatically the right answer. Cornell's point is that the food should be [nutritionally complete and balanced](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feeding-your-cat), meaning it carries an AAFCO-approved nutritional guarantee for the right life stage. Cornell also notes that feeding two or three different foods gives flavour variety, and that you can use whatever mix your cat prefers.

So yes, feeding both is fine for many cats. The trouble in a multi-cat home isn't the food itself. It's keeping the right portions with the right cats.

## Why Mixed Menus Cause Problems in Multi-Cat Homes

### Two foods, one stealing problem

Wet food usually smells stronger than kibble, and in many homes it is the more tempting bowl. So the cat who finishes quickly goes straight for it. That is not misbehaviour. It is a cat doing what cats do. If this sounds like your house, [why cats steal each other's food](/blog/cat-stealing-other-cats-food) explains the motivation.

### Wet food has a clock on it

VCA says that [any canned food not eaten within about 30 minutes should be discarded](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/dry-canned-or-semi-moist-food-choices-for-cats). That sets a hard limit on slow, shy or sore-mouthed cats who need a long, quiet meal. If you stand guard for half an hour, you can manage it. If you are out at work, the wet food either sits there or gets eaten by whoever is bolder.

### Kibble left out invites over-eating

The 2018 AAFP-panel paper on [feline feeding programs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11343346) says modern pet food "is highly palatable and easy to eat rapidly, due to its small chunks and kibble formulation," which can contribute to overeating and weight gain, and that feeding "one or two large meals" can lead to overconsumption and obesity. In a mixed home, the cat on the diet may be the one with the best access to the kibble. Our guide to [free feeding vs scheduled feeding](/blog/free-feeding-vs-scheduled-feeding-multiple-cats) compares the two approaches in more depth.

Think of it as a buffet where one dish is the dessert. Everyone knows where the dessert is, and not everyone has been told to leave it alone.

## Ways to Feed Wet and Dry Food in One Home

None of these is perfect. Pick the one that fits your day.

### Option 1: Everybody gets both, at set times

Give every cat the same pattern: a measured wet meal at fixed times and a measured amount of kibble. This removes the "who is on what" question. It only works if all the cats can eat the same foods. A cat on a prescription, weight-loss or allergy diet can't join in, and our posts on [prescription cat food](/blog/prescription-cat-food-multiple-cats) and [food allergies](/blog/food-allergies-multiple-cats) explain why.

### Option 2: Wet for one cat, dry for the rest, in different rooms

Feed the wet-food cat behind a closed door and take the dish up when she is done or the 30 minutes are up. Feed the kibble cats elsewhere. This costs nothing and it works well if you are at home. The weak point is the door: once opened, any leftovers are fair game. See [how to feed multiple cats separately](/blog/how-to-feed-multiple-cats-separately) for the routine in detail.

### Option 3: Wet food at meals, kibble in puzzle feeders

Some owners keep the day's kibble in puzzle feeders and serve wet food at scheduled times. Puzzle feeders can go badly when one cat dominates them, so read [puzzle feeders for multiple cats](/blog/puzzle-feeders-multiple-cats) first.

### Option 4: A guarded bowl for the cat who needs one

If one cat's food has to be protected, whether it's wet food, a prescription kibble or a slow-eater's meal, a feeder that opens only for that cat removes the supervision job. The feeder doesn't change what the food is, so it doesn't replace the 30-minute rule for wet food, but it does keep the other cats out while the enrolled cat eats.

## Getting the Portions Right When You Mix

Mixing foods makes portions harder, because the labels give calories per can or per cup and cats don't eat in neat units.

1. **Work out the daily total first.** Cornell suggests [asking your veterinarian to help determine the ideal body weight for your cat](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feeding-your-cat), and VCA says to work with your vet team to review the portion of each type of food. Our guide to [working out each cat's portion](/blog/how-much-to-feed-each-cat-multi-cat-home) walks through the sums.
2. **Split that total between wet and dry.** If a cat gets a pouch, subtract its calories from the kibble allowance. Otherwise you are adding the pouch on top.
3. **Weigh the kibble.** A scoop is a guess. The 2018 paper says owners should [make sure their cat is eating an appropriate amount](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11343346), and that portions should be measured.
4. **Count treats.** Cornell suggests treats [should not exceed 10 to 15 percent of daily calories](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feeding-your-cat). If you give them to several cats, [treats for multiple cats](/blog/how-to-give-treats-to-multiple-cats) covers how.
5. **Check shape, not just the bowl.** A cat who slips an extra mouthful here and there won't show it in the bowl. She'll show it on the body. Use a [body condition check](/blog/cat-body-condition-score-multi-cat-home) every few weeks.

For more on handling the soft-food side specifically, including leftovers, see [how to feed wet food to multiple cats](/blog/feed-wet-food-multiple-cats). For a wider look at tools, [portion control methods compared](/blog/cat-portion-control-multiple-cats) is the place to start.

## A Hypothetical Three-Cat Household

Picture Mabel (13, thin, a little sore in the mouth), and two younger cats, Otis and Pip, both a bit round. Mabel needs wet food twice a day and a long, calm meal. Otis and Pip are on measured kibble.

The plan: Mabel eats in the bedroom at 7am and 6pm with the door shut, and her dish comes up after 30 minutes. Otis and Pip get their weighed kibble in the kitchen at the same times, in separate corners. Treats are counted out of their kibble allowance. It's a made-up household, so adjust to your own cats and your vet's advice. The point is that each cat has its own food, place and time, rather than a general "everyone eats at once."

## When This Won't Work

Some of this will not fit your home.

- **If you aren't home at mealtimes,** door-closing routines can't be done. Nothing can guard wet food against the 30-minute limit if you are out.
- **A guarded bowl does not make food last longer.** Wet food left under a lid can still spoil. Ask your vet [how long your cat's food can sit](/blog/how-long-can-wet-cat-food-sit-out-multi-cat-home).
- **Not every cat will use a covered feeder.** Some need slow introduction; [cats afraid of the feeder lid](/blog/cat-afraid-of-feeder-lid) shows how.
- **Medical diets are the vet's call.** If a cat has kidney, urinary, thyroid or diabetic needs, the right mix of wet and dry is a question for your vet, not a blog.
- **If a cat changes eating patterns suddenly,** that needs a vet visit before any new gadget.

## FAQ

### Can I feed my cat both wet and dry food?

Yes, for most healthy cats. Cornell says feeding two or three different cat foods gives flavour variety and you can use whichever mix your cat prefers, as long as the foods are nutritionally complete and balanced. Ask your vet how to split the daily portion.

### Is wet food or dry food better for cats?

Neither is better for every cat. Wet food has far more water (at least 75 percent versus six to 10 percent for dry, per Cornell), while some dry diets carry dental claims, according to VCA. Your vet can advise based on your cat's age, weight and health.

### How long can wet cat food sit out?

VCA says any canned food not eaten within about 30 minutes should be discarded. Check the can or your vet's instructions for any other storage advice.

### How do I stop my other cats eating the wet food?

Separate them in time or space: feed the wet-food cat in a closed room and lift the dish afterwards, or use something that physically limits who can reach the bowl. Supervised feeding works but only while you are there.

### Should I feed all my cats the same food to keep it simple?

It's simpler, but only suitable if every cat can eat that food. Cats on prescription or special diets should stay on theirs. Talk to your vet before switching everyone.
