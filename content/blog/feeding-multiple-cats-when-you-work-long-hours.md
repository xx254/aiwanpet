You get home at 7:15pm and the kitchen tells the story before you have taken your coat off. One bowl is licked clean and pushed halfway across the floor. The other is still full, untouched. Two cats are sitting by the cupboard looking at you as if you have been gone a week, and you have no idea which one ate what.

If you work long hours and share your home with more than one cat, feeding is a puzzle with a missing piece: you. Most advice assumes someone is standing in the kitchen to referee, lift the dish after 30 minutes and notice who walked away early. This post is about feeding multiple cats when you work long hours, and what you can realistically set up so each cat eats its own food while you are out.

---

## Why Working Hours Make Multi-Cat Feeding Harder

### The advice assumes you are home

Standard guidance asks for a lot of presence. A 2018 AAFP-panel paper on [feline feeding programs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11343346) says the daily food allowance should be "split into multiple small meals and fed throughout the 24 h period." Cornell, by contrast, says that [once a cat is adult, feeding once or twice a day is appropriate in most cases](https://vet.cornell.edu/departments/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/how-often-should-you-feed-your-cat). Those two positions are not the same, and your vet can tell you where your own cats fall between them. For a person out ten or eleven hours, the practical question is how to cover that gap without chaos.

### Competition does not pause while you are out

The same AAFP-panel paper notes that in multi-cat households, "competition for food can cause conflict and pre-feeding aggression." It also describes cats as animals that "prefer to eat often and alone." Put those together with an empty house and the fastest, boldest cat is simply left in charge of the bowls. It is not malice. It is the buffet rule: whoever gets there first eats the most.

### You lose your own information

When you are home you notice things: who skipped dinner, who is eating faster than usual. When you are out, you only see the aftermath. That matters, because a change in appetite is often the first sign something is wrong. Our guide to working out [which cat skipped a meal](/blog/cat-not-eating-multi-cat-home) covers how to rebuild that picture.

## Four Ways to Cover a Long Day

None of these is perfect. Most working owners end up combining two.

### 1. Shift the meals to bookend your day

The simplest option is a measured breakfast before you leave and a measured dinner when you return, with a wet or kibble top-up if your vet agrees. It costs nothing, and you can watch each cat eat. The weakness is the long middle stretch, and cats who are used to hungry mornings may become noisy about it. If that is you, see [how to stop a cat waking you for food](/blog/cat-wakes-me-up-for-food).

### 2. Timed feeders, one per cat

Programmable feeders can deliver a small portion at set times. The AAFP-panel paper says that [automatic feeders can be helpful, although they do not typically provide for foraging and predation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11343346). It also notes that meals can be offered through programmable feeders, some of which use individual microchips. The catch for a multi-cat home: a timed feeder dispenses to whichever cat gets there. If you have a fast eater and a slow eater, one feeder per cat in separate spots helps, but it does not stop the quick cat reaching over to the other one. Our comparison of [microchip and automatic feeders](/blog/microchip-vs-automatic-cat-feeder) goes through the differences.

### 3. Puzzle feeders and foraging

The same paper says that using puzzle feeders and hiding kibbles around the home increases activity and provides mental and physical stimulation. This can fill a long afternoon nicely for cats who get on with them. In a home where one cat guards the puzzle, though, it can backfire; read [puzzle feeders for multiple cats](/blog/puzzle-feeders-multiple-cats) before you buy.

### 4. A guarded bowl for the cat whose food has to be protected

If one cat is on a prescription, weight-loss or allergy diet, the question is not just how often they eat but whether anyone else gets to the bowl. A feeder that opens only for the enrolled cat takes the job of supervision off you for that cat's meals. More on that below.

## Set Up the Home So It Needs Less Supervision

### Separate the stations

The AAFP-panel paper recommends that feeding plans include multiple feeding stations that are visually separated. In a flat that can be as simple as one bowl in the kitchen and one in the bedroom behind a half-closed door, or one on the floor and one on a shelf. If space is tight, [feeding multiple cats in a small apartment](/blog/feed-multiple-cats-small-apartment) has layouts that work. Our piece on [why side-by-side bowls fail](/blog/do-cats-prefer-to-eat-alone-multi-cat-home) explains the reasoning.

### Decide what is out and what is not

Cornell says leaving food out all the time may be acceptable for some cats but can lead to overeating and weight gain in others, and that food left out should be replaced with fresh food every day. In a mixed home, that "some cats and others" is the whole problem. If the cat who needs to lose weight has access to the same dry food as the thin senior, free feeding is a trap. [Free feeding vs scheduled feeding](/blog/free-feeding-vs-scheduled-feeding-multiple-cats) lays out the trade-off.

### Keep wet food for when you are there

Wet food has its own clock. VCA says that [canned food not eaten within about 30 minutes should be discarded](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/dry-canned-or-semi-moist-food-choices-for-cats). For a person who is out all day, the realistic answer is to serve wet food in the morning and evening while you are home, and use dry food for the middle of the day. Our post on [feeding wet food to multiple cats](/blog/feed-wet-food-multiple-cats) covers leftovers.

## A Hypothetical Workday

Picture a two-cat flat. Juno is a sleek four-year-old who eats everything in sight. Biscuit is an eleven-year-old on a measured diet. The owner leaves at 7:30am and returns at 7pm.

At 7am, both cats eat in separate rooms with the doors shut, and the owner lifts both bowls at 7:25. Juno's midday portion is in a puzzle feeder in the hallway. Biscuit's goes in a guarded bowl in the bedroom that only he can open. At 7pm, a second supervised meal, bowls up after half an hour. At the weekend, the owner checks body shape rather than the bowl. It is a made-up household, and your vet should set the real amounts, but the pattern is the point: each cat has its own food, place and time, and the unsupervised hours have a plan.

## Keep Track Without Being There

You cannot watch every meal, so lean on what you can observe.

- **Weigh portions rather than scooping.** The AAFP-panel paper says owners should make sure their cat is eating an appropriate amount. A kitchen scale takes ten seconds. See [how much to feed each cat](/blog/how-much-to-feed-each-cat-multi-cat-home).
- **Check the body, not the bowl.** An empty bowl in a shared home tells you very little. A hands-on [body condition check](/blog/cat-body-condition-score-multi-cat-home) every few weeks tells you more.
- **Watch for sudden changes.** If a cat stops eating or is suddenly ravenous, see your vet before changing the setup.

## When This Won't Work

Be honest about the limits.

- **Long gaps are a problem for some cats.** Cats with diabetes, kidney disease or other conditions may need meals at specific times. If that is your cat, your vet's schedule comes first, and you may need a helper at midday.
- **No equipment replaces a person for a sick or elderly cat.** Anything that makes you unable to see how a cat is eating needs a check-in from a friend, neighbour or sitter. [Cat sitter feeding instructions](/blog/cat-sitter-feeding-instructions-multiple-cats) has a template.
- **Timed feeders can fail.** Jams and power cuts happen. Do not rely on a single device for a cat on medication.
- **A guarded bowl does not stop spoilage.** A lid keeps other cats out, but it does not keep wet food fresh.
- **Not every cat accepts a new lid immediately.** If your cat is wary, [the fear of the feeder lid](/blog/cat-afraid-of-feeder-lid) takes patience.

## FAQ

### How long can I leave my cats alone during the day?

It depends on the cat's age, health and diet, so ask your vet. Cornell says adult cats generally do fine on one or two meals a day, but cats with medical conditions may need set meal times.

### How do I feed two cats different food when I'm at work?

Separate them in space and time. Feed before and after work with the cats in different rooms, then use a protected bowl or a separate room for the daytime portion of the cat whose food needs guarding.

### Are automatic feeders good for multiple cats?

They deliver meals on schedule, but the AAFP-panel paper says they do not typically provide for foraging and predation, and a plain timed feeder cannot tell your cats apart. Microchip or RFID versions can limit access, though that is a different kind of device.

### Should I leave dry food out all day while I'm out?

Cornell says it may be acceptable for some cats but can lead to weight gain in others. With more than one cat, the cat who needs less food is often the one who eats the most, so measure portions instead.

### Can I leave wet food out while I'm at work?

VCA says canned food not eaten within about 30 minutes should be discarded. Serve it when you are home.
