Picture the kitchen floor at dinner. One cat has a saucer of thawed raw food. She eats half, picks up a piece, drops it by the fridge and wanders off. A second cat, who is supposed to be eating cooked food, is already licking the saucer. A third walks through the wet patch and jumps onto the counter. You were only trying to feed one of them something different.

That is the difficulty with raw cat food in a multi-cat home. You may choose it for one cat, but the bowl, floor, fridge and litter trays are often shared. Major U.S. health and veterinary sources advise caution or advise against raw diets altogether. This post covers what they say, what changes when there are several cats, and how to tighten your routine if raw food is still coming into the house.

---

## What the Main Sources Say About Raw Cat Food

It is better to know this before working on logistics.

- The CDC's page on [pet food safety](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/about/pet-food-safety.html) says it does not recommend feeding raw pet food or treats to dogs and cats. It also says that "raw is not necessarily healthier."
- The Cornell Feline Health Center's guide to [feeding your cat](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feeding-your-cat) does not recommend these diets. It says they may contain bacteria, viruses and parasites that can infect cats and their owners, and that scientific evidence of a benefit is currently lacking.
- [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/avoiding-raw-food-in-cats) says feeding raw food to cats is potentially dangerous to the cat, to other pets in the house and to the owner.

### What testing has found

An [FDA page on raw pet food](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/get-facts-raw-pet-food-diets-can-be-dangerous-you-and-your-pet) describes testing done from 2010 to 2012. Of 196 raw dog and cat food samples, 15 were positive for *Salmonella* and 32 for *Listeria monocytogenes*.

That study is older, but the concern has not gone away. In September 2025, Cornell reported on [research into commercial raw cat foods](https://news.cornell.edu/node/331569) that found *Salmonella*, *Cronobacter* and *E. coli* in raw or partially cooked meat products. The report covered foods sold frozen, refrigerated and freeze-dried.

### Frozen and freeze-dried are still raw

This is easy to overlook with treats from a shelf. The CDC says freeze-drying, dehydrating or freezing raw animal protein only reduces the amount of germs. If a pouch of crunchy treats is freeze-dried raw meat, treat it as raw. In a house where everyone gets a few, our post on [giving treats to multiple cats](/blog/how-to-give-treats-to-multiple-cats) can help you keep track of who gets what.

## Bird Flu Has Raised the Stakes

The Cornell Feline Health Center's page on [H5N1 avian influenza and cats](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/h5n1-avian-influenza-and-your-cat) advises owners to refrain from feeding raw meat-based foods and treats, including freeze-dried raw ones, and unpasteurized dairy. It says up to 70% of infected cats die of their infections.

Indoor life does not remove the food route. CIDRAP reported on a [Los Angeles County alert](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/avian-influenza-bird-flu/los-angeles-cat-h5n1-deaths-prompt-new-warning-about-raw-pet-food) about two indoor-only cats from one home that became severely ill and died after eating commercial raw pet food. One tested positive for H5N1; the other was not available for testing. The county health department said vets should strongly advise owners against feeding raw pet food, raw meat, raw poultry or raw dairy.

### Signs that need a phone call

According to Cornell, infected cats often develop fever, loss of appetite and lethargy early on. Signs can quickly worsen to include eye or nose discharge, sneezing, coughing, breathing difficulty and neurological signs.

If you suspect it, Cornell says to isolate the cat from other pets and people and call your vet immediately. Phone before you travel so the clinic can take precautions.

## Why One Raw-Fed Cat Affects Every Cat

Think of raw food like glitter. It starts in one place, but you should not assume it stays there.

### Other cats can be exposed without eating it

VCA says harmful bacteria can be shed in saliva or feces, so other pets can be at risk even if they are not fed raw food. It also notes that cats can carry *Salmonella* in their intestines without showing signs of illness. A cat who looks fine is not proof that nothing is being passed around.

In practical terms, think about every place where your cats' mouths and paws meet: shared water bowls, mutual grooming, litter trays and a bowl that someone else "just checks." Giving each cat a separate food bowl does not take care of that list.

### People are part of the household too

The CDC says raw pet food can make you and your family sick when germs spread around your kitchen and home. It lists these people as more likely to get sick:

- Children under 5
- Adults 65 and older
- People with weakened immune systems
- Pregnant women

It also notes higher risk for some pets, including young pets and those with weakened immune systems or certain health conditions. If anyone in your home, human or cat, fits these descriptions, talk to your doctor or vet. Our advice would be to keep raw food out of that house.

## Cooked Alternatives to Discuss With Your Vet

If you were drawn to raw food for a particular reason, name it. Is one cat picky? Are you worried about weight, digestion or a suspected food sensitivity? Each of these is a better starting point for a vet conversation than the word "raw."

VCA stresses choosing a diet that is nutritionally complete, balanced and cooked. Ask your vet which cooked options suit each cat. When you change, our guide to [switching food with multiple cats](/blog/switch-cat-food-multiple-cats) covers keeping the old and new foods straight.

If a cat is on an elimination trial, do not add or swap foods on your own. See [managing food allergies in a multi-cat home](/blog/food-allergies-multiple-cats) first.

## If You Still Feed Raw: A Stricter Routine

The CDC says to talk to your vet first if you are thinking about feeding raw. If you go ahead anyway, tell your vet, as VCA also advises. The steps below can lower some chances of spreading germs. They do not make raw food safe.

### Before the meal

- **Thaw it cold.** The FDA says to thaw in the refrigerator or microwave, not on the counter or in the sink.
- **Do not rinse it.** The FDA advises against rinsing raw meat, poultry, fish and seafood.
- **Give it its own space.** As a house rule, keep it in a sealed container below other food in the fridge, labeled with the cat's name. Our guide to [storing food for several cats](/blog/store-cat-food-multi-cat-home) covers labeling by diet.

### During the meal

- **Feed behind a closed door if you can.** Shut the raw-fed cat in an easy-to-wipe room and keep the others out.
- **Stay nearby.** Watch for pieces carried off the dish.
- **End the meal deliberately.** The FDA says to cover and refrigerate leftovers immediately or throw them out safely. The CDC says to throw away leftovers that have been out at room temperature. The simplest rule that fits both: if it has been sitting out, bin it.
- **Skip grazing and timed dispensing.** Do not leave raw food waiting in a bowl or feeder for a cat to turn up.

### After the meal

- **Wash your hands.** The FDA says to use soap and water for at least 20 seconds after handling raw pet food. The CDC says to wash before as well as after.
- **Clean whatever it touched.** That means the dish, utensils, counter, mat and floor. For disinfecting, the FDA says a solution of 1 tablespoon of bleach to 1 quart of water is effective. Clean off food residue first, check that the surface can take bleach, and keep cats away from the solution. Our post on [cleaning bowls in a multi-cat home](/blog/how-to-clean-cat-food-bowls-multi-cat-home) covers the everyday routine.
- **Be careful with faces.** The CDC says to try not to let your pet lick your mouth or face right after eating, and not to let them lick open wounds or broken skin. The FDA also says to wash your hands thoroughly after touching your pet or being licked.

### A hypothetical three-cat evening

Picture a home with Olive, who gets raw food, and Biscuit and Juno, who eat cooked food. Olive eats in the laundry room with the door shut while the other two eat in the kitchen. When she steps away, her owner removes the dish, bins what is left, wipes up the dropped piece, cleans and disinfects, and washes up before opening the door.

This household is invented. Notice how much work one dish creates, and what the closed door cannot fix: Olive still shares a home, and perhaps litter trays and grooming sessions, with the others. If that routine sounds unrealistic on your most tired weeknight, take that seriously.

If someone else feeds your cats, they need to know all of this, and they should agree to handle raw food. Put it in your [cat sitter feeding instructions](/blog/cat-sitter-feeding-instructions-multiple-cats), or ask your vet about a cooked option while you are away.

## When This Won't Work

A routine reduces some contact, but it cannot sterilize raw food or stop a cat from carrying germs. It is a poor fit if your cats roam during meals, carry food away, or live with anyone in the higher-risk groups. It also breaks down if you cannot clear and clean after every serving.

A physical barrier over a bowl has the same limit. It may help with who reaches a dish, but it does nothing about dropped pieces, saliva, litter trays or your hands. It should not be treated as hygiene protection, and it is no reason to leave raw food sitting out. If a cat has eaten raw food and seems unwell, call your vet.

## FAQ

### Can one cat eat raw food while the others eat cooked food?

You can serve different foods, but you cannot assume the risk stays with one cat. VCA says other pets can be at risk through bacteria shed in saliva or feces, even if they never eat the raw food. Separate feeding does not remove that.

### What if my cat ate another cat's raw food?

Remove the rest, clean the area and wash your hands. Note which cat ate it, when, and the product name and lot details if you have them. Call your vet for advice, and do so immediately if any cat shows signs of illness. Check whether the product has been recalled.

### Is freeze-dried raw cat food safer?

Do not assume so. The CDC says freeze-drying only reduces the amount of germs, and Cornell's H5N1 guidance includes freeze-dried raw foods and treats in what it advises owners not to feed.

### Does freezing raw cat food at home make it safe?

No source we used says it does. The CDC says freezing raw animal protein only reduces the amount of germs.

### How long can raw cat food stay in the bowl?

Do not plan on leaving it there. The FDA says to immediately cover and refrigerate what your pet does not eat, or throw it out safely. The CDC says to discard leftovers that have been out at room temperature.
