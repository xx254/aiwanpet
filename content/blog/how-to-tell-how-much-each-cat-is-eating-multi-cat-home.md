You walk into the kitchen and the bowl is half empty. Three cats are sitting nearby, all looking equally innocent. One of them has not been herself this week, but you could not say whether she ate anything today, yesterday, or at all. You refill the bowl, because that is what you do, and the question goes unanswered again.

Knowing how much each cat is eating sounds like it should be easy. In a multi-cat home it is one of the hardest things to know, and it matters, because a change in appetite is often the first visible sign that something is wrong. This post covers why the bowl tells you so little, several ways to track each cat's intake without turning your life into a spreadsheet, and when a change is worth a call to the vet.

---

## Why You Cannot Tell How Much Each Cat Ate

### One bowl hides several appetites

When cats share a bowl, you can see the total that disappeared, but not who ate it. A cat who eats a little less than usual is invisible, because another cat quietly makes up the difference. The bowl looks the same, and the problem goes unnoticed.

### Free feeding makes it worse

Hill's Pet Nutrition notes in its guide to [feeding multiple cats](https://www.hillspet.co.nz/cat-care/nutrition-feeding/feeding-multiple-cats) that "free choice feeding can be problematic in multi-cat situations, particularly because it's not easy to monitor each cat's appetite and food intake." It says that in most multi-cat homes, meal feeding with individual feeding stations is the best approach. If you are weighing up the two styles, [free feeding vs scheduled feeding for multiple cats](/blog/free-feeding-vs-scheduled-feeding-multiple-cats) goes through the trade-offs.

### Cats hide things

Cats are not good at telling you they feel unwell. A shy cat may eat only when nobody is watching, and a cat who is nauseous may simply walk away from the bowl without any drama. You need a way of noticing quiet changes.

## Why It Is Worth Knowing

PetMD's guide on [how to tell when a cat is sick](https://www.petmd.com/cat/general-health/how-tell-when-cat-sick), reviewed by a veterinarian, says that one of the first signs you might see in a sick cat is a change in appetite. It lists nausea, fever, food aversion, gastrointestinal disease and pain in the mouth among the possible causes of decreased appetite, and adds that any significant change in weight can be a sign of an underlying health issue.

PetMD's piece on [feeding cats separately](https://petmd.com/cat/nutrition/feeding-multiple-cats) makes a related point: feeding cats separately makes it easier to notice when one cat is eating more or less than normal.

Appetite is not the only reason to track intake. It also tells you whether a diet plan is working, whether the overweight cat is really eating only her own portion, and whether the thin cat is getting enough. [How much should I feed my cat](/blog/how-much-to-feed-each-cat-multi-cat-home) covers how to set each cat's target. Tracking is how you find out whether she is meeting it.

## Ways to Track Each Cat's Intake

None of these is perfect. Pick the one that fits your home and your patience.

### Method 1: Separate meals, separate places

This is the simplest approach and the one the sources above point to. Each cat gets her own measured portion in her own spot, at a set time. When the bowl is empty, you know who emptied it. When it is not, you know exactly whose it was.

It does need some space and some supervision. If you can manage a closed door for each meal, so much the better. [How to feed multiple cats separately](/blog/how-to-feed-multiple-cats-separately) lays out the options, including for small homes.

### Method 2: Weigh before and after

A kitchen scale takes the guesswork out of "about a third of a cup." Weigh the portion going in and, if you want to be thorough, what is left when the cat walks away. The difference is what she ate. [Why a scale beats a scoop](/blog/weigh-cat-food-scale-vs-scoop-multi-cat-home) explains how to do this without it becoming a chore.

You do not need to do it every meal forever. A week of careful weighing gives you a baseline for what normal looks like for each cat.

### Method 3: A simple log on the fridge

Keep a sheet of paper and a pen where you feed the cats. After each meal, mark one of three things for each cat: ate all, ate some, ate none. That is enough. You are not trying to measure to the gram. You are trying to see a pattern, such as "Juniper has left some of her dinner four nights running."

If several people feed the cats, a shared log also stops the classic situation where everyone assumes someone else saw her eat.

### Method 4: Watch the other end

Intake is only half the picture. Notice who is in the litter tray, who is drinking, who is vomiting and who is sleeping more. The post on [which cat is throwing up](/blog/which-cat-is-throwing-up-multi-cat-home) covers how to identify the cat when the evidence is on the carpet.

### Method 5: Check the cat, not just the bowl

Food records show what went in. Weight and body shape show what is happening over time. [How to check each cat's body condition](/blog/cat-body-condition-score-multi-cat-home) gives you a hands-on check you can repeat monthly. PetMD notes that older cats may also lose muscle mass and look thinner overall, which is easy to miss when you see the cat every day.

## Signs a Change Is Worth Acting On

A cat who skips one meal on a hot day is probably fine. Patterns matter more than single events. Things worth noting in your log:

- A cat who eats noticeably less than usual for more than a day or two
- A cat who suddenly eats much more than usual, or seems ravenous but is losing weight
- Eating that stops partway, such as walking up to the bowl, sniffing, and leaving
- Dropping food, chewing on one side, or pawing at the mouth
- Eating changes alongside vomiting, diarrhoea, low energy or hiding

PetMD notes that signs such as these should lead to a call to your vet, and that some symptoms are emergencies needing immediate care. If you are unsure which category yours falls into, phoning your vet's practice is a reasonable first step. [My cat isn't eating](/blog/cat-not-eating-multi-cat-home) goes through how to work out which cat skipped a meal and what to do next.

When you do call, a log is useful. "She left about half of each meal since Tuesday, and drank more than usual" is a lot more helpful to a vet than "she seems off."

## A Hypothetical Week

Picture a three-cat home. Poppy is a bold young cat who eats fast. Walnut is middle-aged and a little round. Tilly is a quiet older cat who has always been a slow eater. They share a bowl in the hallway, and the owner refills it each morning.

One week, the owner starts a log. She puts Tilly's meals in the bedroom with the door closed and writes down what is left. By Friday, a pattern has appeared: Tilly leaves some of each meal, every day. Under the old system, the hallway bowl would have been empty by evening, because Poppy and Walnut would have finished it. Nothing would have looked wrong.

This is invented, but it shows the point. Tilly's change was visible only because she was fed separately and someone wrote it down. The owner now has something specific to tell the vet.

## When This Won't Work

Tracking has limits, and it is worth being honest about them.

- **It will not diagnose anything.** A log tells you that something changed. Only a vet can tell you why.
- **It takes some effort.** Weighing and logging every meal is tiring for many households. A short burst to find a baseline, then occasional spot-checks, is more realistic.
- **Separate feeding needs a place and a routine.** In a very small home, or one with a cat who bolts from her own spot to someone else's, you may still find cats sharing. Watch mealtimes at first.
- **Grazers and hunters muddy the picture.** If a cat gets food elsewhere, such as from a neighbour, a treat drawer or a dog's bowl, the log will under-count what she eats.
- **Cats are individuals.** Some eat little and often, and some eat everything at once. What matters is a change from that cat's own normal.

## FAQ

### How do I know which of my cats is not eating?

Feed each cat separately for a few meals and note whether she finishes. Without separation, you can only see the total. [My cat isn't eating](/blog/cat-not-eating-multi-cat-home) has more detail on narrowing it down.

### How long can a cat go without eating before I call the vet?

Sources vary and depend on the cat's age and health, so there is no safe number to give here. PetMD advises that a change in appetite is often an early sign of illness and that you should call your vet if you are concerned. If your cat is also vomiting, very lethargic or showing other worrying signs, do not wait.

### Is free feeding bad for tracking food intake?

It makes it harder. Hill's Pet Nutrition says it is not easy to monitor each cat's appetite and food intake with free choice feeding in multi-cat situations, and recommends meal feeding at individual stations for most multi-cat homes.

### Do I need to weigh every meal?

No. A week or two of weighing gives you a baseline, and after that a log or simple observation is usually enough. Check each cat's weight and body shape regularly, because that shows long-term trends better than any one meal.

### What if two cats eat at the same speed and swap bowls?

Feed them in separate rooms or behind a closed door until they finish, then pick the bowls up. Cats that swap are often the reason a log goes wrong.
