On day three of the switch, the kitchen counter has four labelled containers, a measuring spoon crusted with two kinds of pâté, and a sticky note that says "Bean: 50/50, Pepper: 75/25, Miso: OLD ONLY." You put the bowls down, turn around for the water, and turn back to find Pepper's face in Miso's bowl and Bean sniffing the new food like it might bite him. The note says the plan. The bowls are doing something else.

If you are trying to work out how to switch cat food with multiple cats, this is the usual problem. The standard advice of mixing a little more new food into the old each day assumes one cat eating from one bowl. In a house with two, three or five cats, nobody's ratio stays the same once the bowls go down. Some cats are switching, some aren't, and the slowest eater sets the pace for everyone.

This guide covers the standard transition schedule, how to adapt it when several cats share a kitchen, and when a cat who won't eat the new food is a real problem.

---

## The Standard Way to Switch Cat Food

For a single healthy cat, the method is well established. PetMD, in an article written by a veterinarian, says that ["it generally takes seven to 10 days to switch cat food,"](https://www.petmd.com/cat/nutrition/how-to-switch-cat-food) and gives a schedule like this:

| Days | Old food | New food |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 75% | 25% |
| 3–4 | 50% | 50% |
| 5–6 | 25% | 75% |
| 7 onward | 0% | 100% |

The same article notes that some cats "may require a longer transition period if they show signs of digestive upset," [such as vomiting, diarrhoea or loss of appetite](/blog/which-cat-is-throwing-up-multi-cat-home). If that happens, stay at the current step for a few more days, or go back one step, before moving on.

Mix the two foods together rather than serving them side by side. A cat given a choice will usually just eat the familiar one.

### Wet to dry, dry to wet

Changing format is a bigger change than changing brand. PetMD points out that "wet food is mostly water, so you will need a larger volume of it," and that dry food "is more calorie-dense, so your cat will likely need to be fed less food." A cup of kibble and a cup of wet food are not the same meal. Check the calories on both labels, and ask your vet to confirm the daily amount for each cat before you start. Our guide on [how to feed wet food to multiple cats](/blog/feed-wet-food-multiple-cats) covers the portion and waste side of wet feeding in more detail.

## Why a Food Switch Goes Wrong in a Multi-Cat Home

With one cat, the ratio in the bowl is the ratio the cat eats. With several, three things break it.

### Cats eat from each other's bowls

If one cat finishes fast and moves on to the next bowl, the careful 50/50 mix becomes a 75/25 mix for one cat and a skipped meal for another. Over a week, that undoes the whole point of a gradual change. A fast eater might get a full serving of new food on day two, and the cat who was pushed away might never get past 25%.

### Not every cat is switching

Often only one cat needs the new food: a kidney diet, a weight-loss food, a kitten food for the new arrival. Everyone else stays on what they had. Now there are two foods in the house permanently, and the cats who aren't meant to have the new one will often find it more interesting than their own. Our post on [prescription cat food in a multi-cat home](/blog/prescription-cat-food-multiple-cats) goes into why that matters when the new food is a therapeutic diet.

### The other cats change how a cat eats

Cornell's Feline Health Center lists [the presence of other animals](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feeding-your-cat) among the things that can put a cat off eating, along with noise, heavy traffic, dirty bowls and nearby litter boxes. The AAFP's consensus statement on [feline feeding programs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11343346) notes that in multi-cat homes, "competition for food can cause conflict," and that "cats may not show overt signs of tension or stress." A cat who is already uneasy at mealtimes and then finds an unfamiliar food in the bowl may simply walk away. With other cats around, you might not notice.

Think of it like trying to wean someone off sugar in their coffee when everyone in the office shares one pot. You can measure your own cup carefully, but the pot is what people actually drink.

## How to Switch Cat Food With Multiple Cats, Step by Step

### 1. Decide who is switching, and write it down

Before you open the new bag, list each cat, what they eat now, what they are moving to, and why. If the change is for medical reasons, confirm the target food and daily amount with your vet. If only one cat is changing, that is a different job from switching the whole household, and the steps below will matter more.

### 2. Stop free feeding for the length of the switch

You cannot run a mixing schedule out of a bowl that sits out all day for anyone to visit. Move to set meals, two or more a day, for at least the transition period. If your cats are used to grazing, our comparison of [free feeding vs scheduled feeding](/blog/free-feeding-vs-scheduled-feeding-multiple-cats) explains how to make that change without a revolt.

### 3. Give each cat a separate, consistent feeding spot

AAFP guidance recommends "multiple feeding stations that are visually separated," and that cats "should be fed in locations where they feel safe." During a food switch, this also lets you see what each cat is eating. Keep each cat in the same spot every meal, so if a bowl is untouched you know whose it was.

### 4. Prepare each bowl to its own ratio

Mix each cat's bowl separately, even if two cats are on the same step. Label bowls or use different colours. Bowls for cats on different steps should never sit side by side. Put a wall, a doorway, or at least some distance between them.

### 5. Supervise, then pick the bowls up

Stay in the room for the meal. When a cat walks away, pick up the bowl. Leaving it out for "later" is how the 75/25 bowl ends up in the wrong cat. If you have a cat who eats in several small sittings, you may need to feed that cat behind a closed door for the transition.

### 6. Let each cat move at their own pace

Do not move the whole group to the next step on the same day just because it's easier to prepare. If one cat is on day five and happy while another has soft stools at 50/50, keep the second cat at 50/50 for a few more days. Write the steps down. It is easy to lose track by day four.

### 7. Keep everything else the same

The food is the change. Keep mealtimes, bowls and feeding spots as they were. Ideally don't start a food switch in the same week as a move, a new cat, or a stay at the boarding cattery.

## Special Cases

### Switching to a prescription or therapeutic diet

If the change is for a medical reason, follow your vet's plan. Timing matters here. Meghan E. Burns, DVM, writing for [dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/trigger-warning-recognizing-and-removing-triggers-feline-anorexia), advises: "Don't change a cat's food while hospitalized. This can cause food aversion associated with stress, pain or nausea." Instead, she recommends waiting "until it is home and feeling better to start a gradual change to a new diet." If your cat has just come home from the vet and is still unwell, ask whether to keep the old food for now and start the switch once appetite is back. For kidney diets specifically, see [My Cat Needs a Kidney Diet](/blog/kidney-diet-cat-multiple-cats).

Some diets, such as elimination diets for food allergy, only work if the cat eats nothing else at all. Your vet may ask for a quicker or stricter change. In a multi-cat home, that makes keeping the other food away from that cat the main task. Our [food allergies guide](/blog/food-allergies-multiple-cats) covers how to manage that.

### Switching the whole household to a new brand

This is the easiest version, because every bowl holds the same mix. You can prepare one batch for each meal and split it. Watch for the one cat who eats noticeably less once the new food goes in. Group switches hide a picky eater well, because the bowls still come back empty. Someone else may be finishing them.

### Kitten food and adult food

A kitten moving to adult food and an adult cat stealing kitten food are a two-way version of the same problem. Kitten food is richer, and adults tend to love it. See [How to Feed Cats at Different Life Stages in One Home](/blog/feeding-cats-different-life-stages) for how to keep the two apart.

## When a Cat Won't Eat the New Food

Some resistance is normal. Cats are often wary of unfamiliar food, and Cornell notes that feeding "two or three different cat foods provides flavor variety and may prevent your cat from developing an exclusive preference for a single food." A cat who has eaten one food for years may need longer than seven days.

If a cat is eating less, go back to the last step they ate happily and stay there for several more days. Try warming wet food slightly, or try a different texture of the same diet if one exists.

What you should not do is wait it out. Cats are not like dogs who will eventually give in and eat. VCA Animal Hospitals explains that [hepatic lipidosis](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/fatty-liver-syndrome-in-cats), a serious liver disease, typically follows "a period of anorexia (little or no eating) for three to four consecutive days," and that the risk is greater "if the cat was overweight or obese before the anorexia began." PetMD advises calling your vet right away if a cat has not eaten in 24 hours or seems unusually lethargic.

In a multi-cat home, the hard part is knowing that a cat hasn't eaten. If the bowls are empty, ask yourself whether you actually watched that cat eat. If you didn't, you don't know. Our post on [signs your cat is eating another cat's food](/blog/signs-cat-eating-another-cats-food) lists what to look for.

## When This Won't Work

Separate stations and careful mixing take time at every meal, usually two or three times a day for one to two weeks. If you work long shifts or someone else feeds the cats some days, the schedule will slip, and that's fine as long as you notice when it does. Some cats will not accept a particular food however slowly you go. If a cat on a vet-recommended diet keeps refusing it, ask your vet about alternatives. Many therapeutic diets come in more than one brand or texture. And if two cats fight at every meal, no transition plan will fix that. Deal with the conflict first.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to switch cat food?

Usually seven to ten days, moving from 25% new food to 100% in steps. Cats with sensitive stomachs, older cats, or cats changing from dry to wet (or the reverse) may need longer. Follow your vet's timeline for prescription diets.

### Can I switch all my cats to a new food at the same time?

Yes, if they are all moving to the same food and none has a medical reason to go slower. Prepare one mix per meal, but still watch that each cat eats their own share. Slow down for any cat who has vomiting, diarrhoea or eats less.

### What if only one of my cats needs the new food?

Run the transition for that cat alone. Feed them in a separate spot at set times, pick up bowls after meals, and keep the other cats' food where the switching cat can't reach it, and the reverse. This continues after the switch is done, so it is worth setting up properly.

### My cat refuses the new food. Should I let them go hungry until they eat it?

No. Cats that go several days without eating can develop serious liver problems. Go back to a ratio your cat accepts and progress more slowly. If your cat hasn't eaten for 24 hours, call your vet.

### Should I mix the foods or serve them in separate bowls?

Mix them. Separate bowls let a cat pick the familiar food and ignore the new one, and in a multi-cat home, another cat may eat the bowl that was left.
